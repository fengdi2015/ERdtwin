Clinic marketing & promotion strategy

A practical launch plan for a rheumatology-focused clinic. Set an owner and due date for each action, then track results monthly.

1. Foundation & patient access
  • Publish a fast, accessible website with services, clinician bios, insurance participation, location, parking, accessibility information, and contact options.
  • Keep the Google Business Profile accurate: hours, phone, entrance photos, map pin, holiday changes, and appointment link.
  • Offer a clear new-patient path: referral instructions, what to bring, records process, cancellation policy, and a secure scheduling or contact route.
2. Referral-network growth
  • Create a one-page referral kit for primary care, orthopedics, dermatology, nephrology, and other relevant partners: accepted plans, referral criteria, urgent-path instructions, fax/e-referral route, and expected response time.
  • Schedule concise outreach with nearby practices and hospital discharge teams; provide a named referral contact.
  • Report referral-to-appointment turnaround, no-show rate, and closed-loop referral feedback to improve partner confidence.
3. Patient education & community
  • Use clinician-reviewed, plain-language content on arthritis symptoms, infusion preparation, medication safety, mobility, and visit preparation. Avoid diagnosis or individualized care in public posts.
  • Promote educational events, local support resources, and accessibility features without using patient stories or photos unless properly authorized.
  • Ask for honest public reviews only through a neutral, voluntary process; never expose patient information in a response.
4. Campaigns, measurement & operations
  • Use a small set of campaigns: local search, referral outreach, new-patient education, and reactivation of appropriately contacted former patients.
  • Measure source, inquiry-to-booking conversion, time to appointment, show rate, capacity by service, cost per booked patient, and patient experience.
  • Connect campaigns to operations: ensure room, staffing, infusion capacity, supplies, and front-desk scripts can support the volume before scaling.
5. Pharmaceutical-representative visits
  • Use appointment-only visits, coordinated through one staff owner; do not allow drop-ins to interrupt patient care or waiting areas.
  • Meet in the Admin room or a designated non-patient space, not exam, infusion, storage, or reception areas. Keep patient records, screens, and conversations out of sight and earshot.
  • Maintain a visitor log with organization, representative name, date, staff host, purpose, and materials received. Require visible visitor identification and follow the clinic's sign-in and escort policy.
  • Set a written policy for samples, meals, gifts, educational items, and product discussions; route all materials through clinical leadership and applicable organizational or legal review.

Room-based photo, poster & information placement plan

ReceptionPrimary promotion zonePlace one large, calming clinic or community photo behind/near check-in, plus a single services poster at adult eye level. Use a neat brochure holder for new-patient, referral, accessibility, and event information. Keep the desk front, circulation path, and any PHI screens clear.
CorridorWayfinding onlyUse small, consistent room signs and one narrow health-education frame only where it does not narrow the route. Avoid freestanding displays, floor stands, or anything that reduces wheelchair travel width.
Exam roomsPatient educationUse one wipeable framed poster or digital display on the wall visible from the exam table: arthritis self-management, visit preparation, hand hygiene, or medication safety. Do not place materials behind the door swing, over a sink, or where they conflict with clinical equipment.
InfusionComfort & safetyUse calming artwork or a quiet digital information screen in the patient sightline. First map and test every outlet; reserve accessible power for recliners/pumps and equipment, with no cords crossing travel paths. Keep emergency equipment, staff sightlines, outlets, infusion equipment, and cleanable surfaces unobstructed. Avoid promotional materials implying a specific treatment outcome.
Consult / physician officeProfessional credibilityDisplay credentials, affiliations, or a neutral clinic mission piece behind the clinician—not visible on an unsecured screen or through an open door. Keep patient-facing education in a small holder, separate from protected documents.
Admin & StorageStaff-only supportUse internal campaign calendars, referral workflow sheets, rep-visit policy, and inventory/reorder notices. Do not place patient-facing promotional signs here; keep storage shelving, exits, and work surfaces usable.
Bathroom & KitchenMinimal / no promotionBathroom: only hygiene, accessibility, and emergency/contact notices using cleanable, securely mounted material. Kitchen: staff notices only. Do not use either room for patient promotions or product marketing.
Privacy, compliance & brand safeguards
  • Use only approved systems and vendors for any patient data. Do not put PHI into public forms, ad platforms, pixels, chat widgets, or marketing tools unless your privacy and security review permits it.
  • Do not use patient photos, testimonials, or identifiable stories without the appropriate written authorization. Marketing rules can vary by communication and payment arrangement.
  • Have a privacy official or health-care counsel review campaigns, representative-visit policy, consent language, claims, accessibility, and vendor agreements before launch. See HHS HIPAA authorization guidance.
